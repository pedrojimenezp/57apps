Privacy Policy for Heads Up

Last updated: 5 September 2026

This policy was rewritten on 5 September 2026. The previous version described an app with no accounts, no sync and no sharing, which is what Heads Up was when it was written. It has since gained all three, and that version no longer described the app you have. This one does. Every earlier version is in the public git history at github.com/pedrojimenezp/57apps.

The short version

  • Heads Up works with no account. Used that way, nothing you type leaves your phone.
  • If you make an account, your reminders are copied to a server so they reach your other devices. Their text is stored in ordinary readable form. It is encrypted in transit and at rest, but it is not end-to-end encrypted, and we can read it.
  • There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no tracking of any kind. Not reduced — none.
  • Voice input is transcribed by your phone's own speech recogniser, which on most Android phones is Google's. Heads Up never receives, stores or sends audio.
  • You can delete your account and everything on it from inside the app, immediately.

Who we are

Heads Up is made by 57Apps, a one-person operation. For the purposes of the GDPR, 57Apps is the data controller for the information described here. Contact: contact@57apps.com.

This policy covers the Heads Up mobile app and the pages on www.57apps.com.

Using Heads Up without an account

This is the default, and it is a complete way to use the app. No sign-in is offered on first launch and none is required for any reminder feature.

Used this way, everything lives on your device:

  • Your lists, reminders, notes, tags, due dates and completion history, in a database on the phone.
  • Your settings — notification lead times, how long completed reminders are kept, your voice input language.
  • Three rolling automatic backups of that database, kept in the app's own storage. They are never uploaded anywhere, and they are deliberately excluded from Android's cloud backup.

None of it is transmitted. Uninstalling the app deletes all of it.

One exception worth naming: if you have Android's own backup or device-transfer feature switched on, Android may copy the app's reminder database to your Google account, as it does for other apps. That is Android's feature, not ours, and it is governed by Google's terms. The app's backup rules deliberately limit that copy to the reminder database and nothing else.

When you sign in

Making an account turns on syncing. From then on, the following is stored on our server:

Your identity

  • Your email address.
  • If you signed in with Google or Apple, that provider tells us your email address and gives us an account identifier. We do not receive your password, your contacts, or anything else from them.
  • A password, if you made one — stored only as a hash, by our authentication provider, never in readable form.

Your reminders

  • The text of each reminder and its notes.
  • Due dates and times, and the timezone the due date was set in.
  • Priority, repeat rules, completion state and times.
  • Your list and tag names.
  • Reminder bookkeeping — how many times something has been snoozed, when it last nudged you.
  • Which account created each item, and a random identifier for the installation it was created on.

Your reminder text is readable by us. It is encrypted while it travels and encrypted on the disks it sits on, but it is not end-to-end encrypted — there is no key that only you hold. If that matters for something you were about to write down, use the app without an account, where it never leaves your phone.

The installation identifier

Each installation generates a random 128-bit number the first time it runs, and keeps it. It is used to tell two devices' copies of the same reminder apart during syncing.

It is not derived from your hardware, it is not an advertising identifier, and it is not shared with anyone. Reinstalling the app produces a new one.

Sharing a list with someone

Shared lists need an account, and they involve other people's information as well as yours. Three things about them are easy to miss, so they are here rather than in the small print.

  • Inviting someone stores the address you typed. It is saved on our server so the invitation can be found when that person signs in — including if they have no account and never make one. It stays until someone on the list cancels the invitation or the invited person responds to it. If you have been invited to a list and would rather your address were not there, email us and we will remove it.
  • Everyone on a shared list can see everyone else's email address, including the addresses of people who have been invited but have not yet accepted. This is how the members screen shows you who you are sharing with. Consider it before accepting an invitation from someone you would rather not give your address to.
  • Any member can complete anything on the list. Only the person who added a reminder, or the list's owner, can change its text or delete it. The owner can remove members and delete the list, which removes it for everyone.

Deleting things, and what stays

Deleting a reminder

It disappears from your devices immediately. On the server the row is marked as deleted rather than removed, because that is how your other devices find out it is gone. Its text stays on the server in that state. Nothing removes those marked-deleted rows automatically today; deleting your account removes them.

The "keep completed reminders" setting

Your choice of 30 days, 90 days or forever removes old completed reminders from that device. If you are signed in, it does not delete the server's copy. We would rather say that plainly than let the setting imply something it does not do.

Voice input

The microphone is used only while you have voice input open, and only if you grant the permission.

  • Heads Up never records, stores or transmits audio. It asks your phone's speech recogniser for text and receives text.
  • That recogniser is a part of your operating system, not part of this app. On most Android phones it is Google's, and it may send your audio to Google to transcribe it. When it does, that is covered by Google's privacy policy, not this one. We have no access to it. Some devices and settings do the transcription on the phone instead; your Android settings control that.
  • Adding a reminder through Google Assistant works the same way — the Assistant transcribes it entirely outside the app and hands Heads Up the finished text.
  • Typing always works. Nothing in the app requires the microphone.

Who else is involved

  • Supabase hosts the database and handles sign-in. They process your data on our instructions.
  • Google, if you use Sign in with Google; and indirectly, as described above, through your phone's speech recogniser.
  • Apple, if you use Sign in with Apple. Not offered on Android.
  • GitHub Pages serves this website, and sees the usual request logs including IP addresses.

There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no attribution and no telemetry in this app. No third-party SDK of that kind is present in it. The app writes no diagnostic logs at all in the version you install. This website sets no cookies, runs no analytics and makes no third-party requests — the typeface it uses is served from the same domain as the page.

We do not sell your data, and we do not use it to train anything.

Where your data is

The server is in the United States (AWS us-east-1, Northern Virginia). If you are in the UK or the European Economic Area, your information is transferred there and is handled under the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses, which is the basis our hosting provider operates on.

How long it is kept

Your reminders stay on the server while your account exists, including the marked-deleted rows described above. Deleting your account removes all of it. We do not keep backups of your content beyond what our hosting provider retains for operational recovery, which is measured in days.

Deleting your account

In the app: Settings → Account → Delete account. It is immediate and it cannot be undone. It removes your account, your reminders, your lists, your tags, your marked-deleted rows and any invitations sent to or by you.

Two things are worth knowing before you do it. Reminders you added to lists you share with other people are deleted too — they were yours. And a shared list that you own is not destroyed: it passes to whoever joined it first, so the other people on it keep their reminders.

If you cannot open the app, email contact@57apps.com from the address on the account. Full instructions are on the account deletion page.

Children

Heads Up is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly create accounts for them. The app does not ask your age. If you believe a child has made an account, email us and we will remove it.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to see what we hold about you, correct it, have it deleted, take a copy elsewhere, or object to how it is used. Deletion is self-service in the app; for anything else, email us and we will answer within 30 days.

Where the law asks us to name a basis for processing: syncing your reminders is us performing the service you asked for; the microphone is your consent, which you can withdraw in your phone's settings; keeping the service running and unabused is our legitimate interest.

If you are in the EEA or UK and are unhappy with our answer, you can complain to your national data protection authority.

Security

Traffic is encrypted with TLS. Data is encrypted at rest by our hosting provider. Passwords are hashed, never stored in readable form. The database enforces, per row, that only members of a list can read it — that rule lives in the database rather than in the app, so a bug in the app cannot bypass it.

What we do not claim: end-to-end encryption, or that any system is impossible to breach.

Changes to this policy

If what the app does changes, this page changes with it and the date at the top moves. For anything that materially widens what we collect, we will say so in the app rather than only here — quietly editing this page is how the previous version came to be wrong.
